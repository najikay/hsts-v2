## 1. What a teacher gets

She opens the builder's auto tab, says *five from Recursion, three Hard from anywhere*, and either
gets a paper proposed with points already totalling 100 — savable in one click, T-3.4 — or she is
told exactly what her bank is short of, every shortfall at once:

> Topic "Recursion": requested 1 Hard, bank has 0.

That sentence is F3.3's own example, and the seed carries one deliberately thin topic (Recursion,
two questions, no Hard) so it can be produced on stage without anybody touching the database.
T-3.5 and T-3.6 are the two shots at it.

**Nothing is written.** No exam, no version, no allocated serial. T-3.5's "No exam is created" is
true because there was never a write to undo, not because a rollback worked.

## 2. The contract asserted a guard that was not there ⚑

§7.3a, ruled 2026-08-24, states:

> If any topic quota is present, the course-wide quota may use `any` only. A course-wide quota
> with graded buckets stands alone. **`ExamValidator.quotaProblem` refuses any other combination.**

It did not. `quotaProblem` refused a null quota, a negative bucket, a duplicate topic and an empty
request — and nothing about that combination. The method's own javadoc said so from the other
direction: *"§7.4's selection rule is an open contract question because of it."* Two documents
written by people each reading the other, neither matching the code.

**It is the load-bearing rule, not a tidiness one.** With it, quota pools form a nesting hierarchy:
topic pools pairwise disjoint, a topic's difficulty buckets inside it, the course-wide `any` pool
containing everything. On a nesting family Hall's condition collapses to exactly the per-bucket
comparisons §7.3 already makes — so bucket checking is *complete* rather than approximate, and
most-constrained-first greedy is *exact* rather than merely sensible. Without it: pools cross, no
bucket is short, the request is impossible, §7.3 names no row, `AutoComposeResult` refuses an empty
report, and she meets `INTERNAL` on the one verb F3.3 exists for.

Implemented, and the refusal **names both legal shapes** — your stated condition on accepting the
rule, because "that combination is not allowed" leaves her guessing which half of her grid to
delete.

## 3. What is built

| | |
|---|---|
| `AutoComposer` | the buckets, the report, the selection. FX-free, session-free, writes nothing |
| `ExamBuildRepository.findAutoCandidates` + `AutoCandidate` | the pool: one course, live, latest versions, no answer key |
| `ExamValidator.quotaProblem` | §7.3a's shape rule, plus the points ceiling of 5.3 |
| `ExamService.autoCompose` + `AutoOutcome` | validation, then scope, then one read, then a pure compose |
| `ExamHandlers` | `EXAM_AUTO_COMPOSE` registered — the seventh verb |

**Two design choices worth the review time:**

**One query for the whole course, bucketed in memory.** Not one query per quota. §7.2 property 2
makes `available` a number she is invited to verify by filtering her own bank; if counting and
picking ran as separate queries, a colleague saving a question between them would make the report
describe a bank that no longer exists. One snapshot means the count and the selection are the same
rows by construction.

**Topics are bucketed by `QuestionValidator.sameTopic`, not by SQL.** The pool query scopes by
course only. Filtering by topic in SQL would make the database the authority for the pool while the
service stayed the authority for the quota buckets — two authorities on one question, diverging
silently in Hebrew, which is P-9 exactly.

## 4. The audit, and the three defects it found ⚑

Run after the code existed, per the DoD. **It could not break the completeness claim** — it
attacked the nesting argument four ways and could not construct criteria reaching the
`IllegalStateException` that guards it. Three real defects, all fixed, each now with a test that
was watched failing first.

**4.1 `ExamService.autoCompose` had no test of any kind.** Deleting the `requireTeachesCourse`
call left the whole suite green: `AutoComposerTest` is pure, `ExamHandlersTest` covered only the
role gate, `ExamBuildWiringGuardTest` checks registration rather than scope. **This is the mutation
none of the eight original plants caught**, and it sits on the authorization boundary — every
shortfall's `available` is a real count from the named course, so a teacher able to compose against
a course she does not teach reads that course's topic and difficulty spread straight out of the
report. That is P-5's class, and it is why §3's table gives this verb a *throwing* guard. Four
service tests added; planted the deletion, three of them fail.

**4.2 One topic produced two rows the client renders as the same sentence.** `TopicQuota("Recursion",
1, 0, 0, 3)` emitted `(Recursion, null, 3, 2)` as a leaf and `(Recursion, null, 4, 2)` as the topic
aggregate. §7.1 gives that shape exactly one sentence, so they are indistinguishable on the wire:

> Topic "Recursion": requested 3 questions, bank has 2.
> Topic "Recursion": requested 4 questions, bank has 2.

She reads two problems, or a report that cannot count. Neither the property test nor the duplicate
guard could see it — both rows are individually correct and both have something missing. The
aggregate now supersedes the same-shape row, because it is the number she can act on in one edit;
a graded leaf for the same topic still stands beside it, per §7.2 property 1.

**4.3 A blank grid row made a legal request illegal.** `seenTopics` counted zero-demand rows, so an
empty topic row beside a lone course-wide graded quota tripped the shape refusal — telling her to
delete a row that asks for nothing. A criteria grid always carries blank rows; E7.13's will send
them. It also meant the validator and the composer disagreed about what "a topic quota is present"
means, the composer skipping empty quotas in two places while the validator counted them: two
expressions of one question, checked against each other nowhere.

**Plus a guard nothing defended.** `findAutoCandidates`'s javadoc promises an ordering that §7.5's
reproducibility rests on, and deleting `order by q.displayId` left the whole suite green — MySQL
guarantees no row order without it. The symptom would be a teacher whose "it gave me a strange set"
cannot be reproduced from her seed, which is the one thing §7.5 exists to make possible.

## 6. Three things for you

**6.1 "Exact equality" for topic matching — a clarification, added as a marked note in §7.3
awaiting your confirmation.** §7.3, `TopicQuota.topic`'s javadoc, `BANK_WIRE_CONTRACT` ruling 7.6
and my own `AutoCandidate` all say topic matching is **exact equality**. The implementation folds
case, accents, Hebrew final forms and Yiddish digraphs, via `sameTopic`. I believe the code is
right and the phrase is loose — ruling 7.6 chose option A over a normalising filter, and the filter
it ruled on runs in SQL under `utf8mb4_unicode_ci`, so "exact" meant the column's exactness rather
than Java `equals`. But §7 is DRAFT and that sentence is the one line a client author or a second
query would rely on. I did not edit the sentence, only annotated it, and I did not touch
`TopicQuota` at all because `common/dto/**` is yours.

**6.2 `QuestionValidator.sameTopic`'s javadoc said E7's auto-composer selects with
`qv.topic = :topic`.** It does not, and the correction matters beyond accuracy: `sameAnswer`'s
"stricter is the safe direction" argument **does not carry over to this consumer**. For duplicate
answers the worst case of over-folding is a teacher told two similar answers are too similar. Here
`sameTopic` is the *sole* authority for the bucket while the bank browse screen she checks against
still filters with SQL `=`, so a pair MySQL folds and `sameTopic` keeps apart makes `available`
*smaller* than the count she sees — the §7.2 property 2 failure. Agreement in **both** directions
is what this consumer needs, which is what `BankRoundTripIntegrationTest`'s bidirectional agreement
test already measures. Corrected in both files; raising it because the reasoning changed, not just
the sentence.

**6.3 `Verb.EXAM_AUTO_COMPOSE`'s error list stopped enumerating.** It names three `VALIDATION`
causes; there are now five, with §7.3a's shape refusal and the points ceiling. `common/protocol/**`
is yours, so it travels with this PR rather than in it. An enumerated list that stops enumerating
reads as complete.

## 7. Evidence

- `./mvnw clean verify` on JDK 21 with `HSTS_REQUIRE_MYSQL=true`: **BUILD SUCCESS, 0 failures, 0
  errors, all coverage checks met.** 32 tests added — 18 in `AutoComposerTest`, 5 validator, 4
  service, 1 handler, 4 repository.
- **Nine mutations planted, all caught**: widest-quota-first, `available` net rather than raw,
  first-failure reporting, the shape rule removed, the points ceiling removed, soft-deleted
  questions readmitted, an older version pinnable, the course scope dropped, and the
  authorization guard deleted.
- **Three of those reported "not caught" on the first pass and were bad plants, not gaps** — one
  `sed` targeted a line already rewritten, one inserted an early return *after* the loop that had
  emitted both rows, and `versionNo >= max(...)` is an expensive way to write `=`. Re-planted
  properly, all three caught. Recorded because "eight for eight" would have been the more
  flattering sentence and the wrong one.
- **The load-bearing test is a property, not a case**: over 400 generated bank shapes and criteria,
  every `available` in every report must equal the raw count she would get by filtering her own
  bank to that topic and difficulty, and every shortfall must have something actually missing.
- The audit's own limit, stated: `AutoComposerTest`'s `rawCount` helper compares topics with
  `equalsIgnoreCase`. On the generator's four ASCII topics that agrees with `sameTopic` exactly, so
  the property holds — but it could not detect a divergence of the kind 6.2 describes. Closing that
  needs Hebrew and accented topics in the generator, and is worth doing when 6.1 is ruled.
