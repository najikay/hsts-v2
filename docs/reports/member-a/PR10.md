# E6: the bank's read scope, and the contract corrected for your two-scope ruling

**Two things: `reachesCourse`, and the contract changes your ruling required.** The handlers are
the next PR; this is the last piece they sit on.

## 1. Your ruling, implemented

The read scope is `Authorization.reachesCourse` over a `ReachableCourses` seam. The write gate is
`requireTeachesCourse`, unaltered from PR #20. Each javadoc names the other and states the split's
source: spec §3 and F2.1 for authoring, §7.3 and F4.1/F4.2 for visibility.

`coordinatorReadsWhereSheCannotWrite` is the whole ruling in one test — the same caller, the same

## 2. Three corrections to my own contract, two of which were rules no handler could implement

**§2's role table had one scope where there are two.** It gave a coordinator "every course of the
subject she coordinates" undifferentiated, which silently granted her authoring rights across her
whole subject. Nobody would have noticed until she wrote a question into a colleague's bank.

**§3's guard column named `requireBankRead` on `BANK_LIST`.** That verb has no single course for a
guard to check; it filters by the reachable set. The row described something unimplementable.

**And the three single-question reads needed the boolean form, not a throwing one.** They resolve
the course from a *stored* question, so a refusal naming it is the existence oracle §2 forbids.
Only `QUESTION_CREATE` throws, because it is the one verb where the caller supplies the course
herself and learns nothing she did not already know.

§3 now has a "how scope applies" column rather than a guard column, and §5 states the normalisation
rule that goes with it.

## 3. One deviation from your naming suggestion, and I would rather you overruled it than not see it

You wrote: *"Name the composed one whatever reads best (`requireBankRead` or
`requireCourseVisible`)."* **I built it and then deleted it.** What ships is the boolean
`reachesCourse` and no throwing sibling.

The reason is not style. Post-implementation audit found that the throwing form had **no call site
in §3 at all** — every read verb uses the boolean — while being the one-line way to do the wrong
thing next to the three-line way to do the right one: call the boolean, map false to `NOT_FOUND`,
plumb the code back. A handler author reaching for the obvious method named after the section
heading reconstructs the P-5 oracle, and **no test catches it**, because the guard behaves exactly
as its own tests pin it to behave.

METHOD rule 14 is that the honest way past a guard should be cheaper than the dishonest way.
Documenting the trap is weaker than removing it. **If you want the name back it is ten lines**, and
I would rather add it when a verb needs it than ship it because it was in a message.

## 4. What the audit found, all four mine

Run before this PR opened, per the P-6 gate. Every finding verified in the file before acting.

### 4.1 My seam signature made F9.3 unimplementable

`ReachableCourses.forUser(long)` took a bare user id. It is the **only one of the three directories
whose answer depends on the caller's role**: "does she teach course 11" and "does she coordinate
subject 10" are facts about rows, but "which courses may she read" is not, because a `PRINCIPAL`
reaches every course.

**And she has no `course_teachers` rows and no `coordinators` rows.** So the union §3 specifies
returns her nothing, and F9.3's read-only browse shows an empty bank at the defense.

Now `forCaller(CallerContext)`. Two call sites and six test lambdas today; after seven handlers it
would have been the filter, the per-request memo, four verbs and the contract text.

**This is the same shape as the empty-bank finding from the pre-build audit** — a role whose scope
the query cannot express — built again one layer down, hours later, by the person who had just
fixed it.

### 4.2 Two javadoc claims that were false, one of them next to the rule forbidding it

The seam said *"the production implementation says so by role rather than by query"* — there is no
production implementation. And *"Wired from `HSTSServer`'s assembly beside its two siblings"* — it
is called from tests only.

Sixty lines above, on the sibling, written the same afternoon: *"claiming a wiring that does not
exist is the defect P-6 is about and the claim had to expire when the fact did."* I wrote the rule
and broke it in the next method. Both now say "not wired yet", and the wiring line is §6.

### 4.3 `reachesCourse` had lost its sibling's role-check clause

`teachesCourse` documents its caller as *"already role-checked by the caller of this method"*;
mine did not. The behaviour is identical and the split is correct — deciding who may call a verb is
the handler's job — but the one place an author learns that was missing on the read side, **where
the payload is a `QuestionDetail` carrying the answer key** and no structural guard can see a
missing call.

### 4.4 A mutation my tests could not catch

A silent `if (directory == null) directory = REACHABLE_COURSES.get();` left every test green,
because the field's default is `UNWIRED` and `@AfterEach` resets it there. The explicit-directory
form's entire purpose is depending on nothing global.

`nullDirectoryDoesNotFallBackToGlobalState` installs a **permissive** directory first, which is
what makes the fallback visible. Planted and watched failing; it was the only test that moved.

## 6. One line for you, since `HSTSServer` is yours

```java
// beside useCourseTeachers and useSubjectCoordinators
Authorization.useReachableCourses(caller -> caller.hasAnyRole(Role.PRINCIPAL)
        ? Transactions.inTx(sessionFactory, session -> courses.findAllCourseCodes(session))
        : Transactions.inTx(sessionFactory, session -> {
              Set<String> reachable = new LinkedHashSet<>(
                      courses.findTaughtCourseCodes(session, caller.userId()));
              reachable.addAll(courses.findCoordinatedCourseCodes(session, caller.userId()));
              return Set.copyOf(reachable);
          }));
```

**`findAllCourseCodes` does not exist yet** and is mine — it lands with the handlers, which is when
the first caller appears. Until the wiring exists `reachesCourse` refuses everything, which is
correct for an unwired guard and is pinned by a test, and nothing calls it yet.

**Not `CourseRepository.findForUser`**, which merges taught with **enrolled**: under it a student's
reachable set would be her enrolments, `reachesCourse` would answer true, and the only thing
between her and an answer key would be a `requireRole` call in each handler. The write-side sibling
carries the same warning; the read seam now does too.
