### 2.2 What the amendment got wrong before the audit, and now does not

Two corrections, both caught by the cold read, both verified by me against the code before acting:

## 3. Volunteered, with reversal costs

### 3.1 The TestFX scroll guard — and the defect I introduced fixing it ⚑

**The original defect.** `ExamBuilderInteractionTest` had a `clickOnNode` helper whose javadoc said
"every click in this file goes through here". True when written; false by 2026-08-26, because the
Generate button in the auto tab called `clickOn` directly. Latent, not live — it passed at
`a0b3097`. I am not claiming I fixed a breaking test.

**My first repair was the same defect one level up.** I overrode
`clickOn(Node, MouseButton...)` and wrote that a caller "cannot reach the unscrolled click". The
cold read refuted it and I confirmed with `javap -c` against `testfx-core-4.0.18`: that signature
is an **interface default whose whole body forwards** to the abstract
`clickOn(Node, Motion, MouseButton...)`. `clickOn(node, Motion.DIRECT)`, `doubleClickOn(node)` and
`rightClickOn(node)` all still reached an unscrolled click. **I replaced a false guarantee with a
different false guarantee and wrote it into a javadoc arguing the reader need not check.**

**The fix that is actually structural.** The hook is now `point(Node)` — abstract on the interface,
and the point every node-targeted method resolves its target through. Verified rather than assumed:
`clickOn(Node, Motion, …)` and `doubleClickOn(Node, Motion, …)` both call `point(Node)` as their
first instruction. It is also the honest semantics — `point(Node)` means "aim at this node", and
aiming at a node the screen cannot show is the bug. The javadoc now names what is **not** covered
(the query forms, `clickOn("#id")` and friends), because an absolute is what got me twice.

**Planted, both times.** Removing the scroll fails **6 of 16** — three loudly with
`BoundsLocatorException`, three *silently* as assertions against screens the robot never touched.
Identical counts before and after the move to `point(Node)`, so the change lost no coverage and
gained the other routes.

That plant also disproved a comment I would otherwise have left: line 236 claimed the picker click
was reachable "only because the harness sizes the window past the whole form". With the scroll
removed, that exact line throws. Corrected and dated.

*Reversal: delete the override and restore a helper. The javadoc corrections should outlive it
either way.*

## 6. The lesson, and where it went

**A fix for a falsified comment is the most likely place to write a new one.** The author has just
proved to himself that he understands the mechanism, and that confidence is what produces the
absolute. Both of my false claims — "a caller cannot reach the unscrolled click" and "there is no
server sentence for it" — were written *while correcting someone else's overreach*, in the same
hour, with the evidence for the correction fresh.

Method rule 5's tell already names it: a sentence that quantifies — *every*, *cannot*, *no* —
beside a mechanism that has grown. What this round adds is *when* to look for it, which is the
moment you feel most entitled to write one. That belongs in rule 5 and I will add it there.

The second lesson is smaller and was in the handoff rather than the method: **check the build
result, not a grep of its output.** A pipe to `tail` returns `tail`'s exit code, and the first
compile failure of this round reported `EXIT=0` while `[ERROR] COMPILATION ERROR` sat three lines
above it. Every build in this PR was judged on `BUILD SUCCESS`.
