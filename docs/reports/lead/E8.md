## 1. What this epic is actually fixing

Two of v1's failures meet here, and the second one is the ⚑ on E8.4.

**The coordinator could not see the exam she was approving.** The obvious repair is "build a preview
screen". That repair fails the same way v1 did, a semester later: a screen that renders its own idea
of a paper looks right on the day it is written and drifts the first time either side changes a
padding, a wrap or an image size, and the drift is invisible until it is on a projector.

So the fix is not a screen, it is a **shared code path**, in three places at once:

| layer | shared thing | before E8 | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| repository | `QuestionRepository.findForTakeExam` (no-correctness projection) | E10 only | E8 preview too |
| server mapper | `ExamPaper.toWire(TakeExamQuestion)` | private method on `AttemptService` | extracted class, used by `AttemptService` **and** `ApprovalService` |
| wire type | `common.dto.exam.ExamQuestion` | student only | carried unchanged inside `ExamPreview` |
| client component | `client.features.exam.QuestionCardView` | inline in `ExamFormView` | extracted, used by `ExamFormView` **and** `ExamPreviewView` read-only |

"The coordinator sees exactly what the student sees" is therefore a statement about the types and the
node graph, not a promise about two files. `ApprovalInteractionTest.previewRendersTheStudentPaper`
checks it by looking at the scene graph — two `.question-card`, eight `.question-option`, the option
texts — rather than by trusting a DTO.

**An approval was not bound to anything in particular.** Every decision here is guarded twice inside
one transaction: the version must still be `PENDING`, and its `lock_version` must still be the one
the coordinator's screen was rendered from. Details in §3.

## 2. Decisions the lead should double-check

Ordered by how much I would want a second opinion.

### 2.1 `MY_APPROVALS_GET` — a fifth verb, in E7's territory ⚑

The brief named four verbs. I added a fifth. **Why:** E8.6 requires the rejection reason to be
"visible on the exam", and `NotificationCatalog.approvalRejected` already points at route id `exams`
— which had no screen, because E7 has not landed. A notification whose click does nothing is half a
feature, and F4.2's second clause is not satisfiable by a bell alone.

**How I kept it out of E7's way:** the verb and its payload are named after *approvals*, not after
exams (`MY_APPROVALS_GET` → `MyApprovals`), it returns only approval state and rejection reasons, and
the contract records that E7's exam list absorbs it and this verb retires. The screen behind route id
`exams` (`MyApprovalsView`) is likewise explicitly a placeholder that E7 replaces.

**What to check:** that you are happy with a temporary screen at E7's route id, and that Member A
knows both facts. The rail item's tooltip says "The exam builder arrives with E7" so the label does
not over-promise.

### 2.2 The `versionSubmitted` hook does three things, not two ⚑

The brief said E7's submit emits the `APPROVAL_REQUESTED` notification and E8 does the supersede. I
folded the notification into the same hook. **Why:** two emit points across an epic boundary means
E7 can emit a request for a version whose supersede failed, or emit the two notifications in the
order that reads backwards ("a newer version replaced one in your queue" *after* "an exam is waiting
for you"). One method cannot do either.

**The line I drew:** E7 owns the `DRAFT → PENDING` transition; E8 owns everything the queue sees.
`ApprovalService.versionSubmitted(examVersionId)` supersedes, notifies about the supersede, and
notifies about the request. **Member A must therefore not emit an approval notification in E7.6.**
It is in the contract, in the TODO note, and in §6 below.

### 2.3 `PreviewAnswerRow` rather than reusing `AnswerReviewRow`

The brief said to use the `AnswerReviewRow`/`ForAuthoring` conventions. I followed the convention and
not the shape, and this is the one place I read the instruction as naming a principle rather than a
class.

`AnswerReviewRow` is a **marked paper**: `chosen`, `isCorrect`, `pointsAwarded`. Nothing has been sat
in a preview — the exam has never been given to anybody — so reusing it means sending `chosen = null`,
`isCorrect = false` and `pointsAwarded = 0` for every question, and the first reader to take those at
face value is reading a lie about an exam with no attempts. `PreviewAnswerRow` is three components
and pairs by `questionVersionId` with the `ExamQuestion` already on the wire, so the coordinator's
paper is not duplicated in a second shape that could disagree with it.

What I did keep is the discipline that record established: **one place correctness is serialized per
audience, named so the audience is visible, with a tested guard in front of it.** The repository read
is `findAnswerKeyForAuthoring`, and `CorrectnessLeakGuardTest`'s spelled-out inventory now names it.

### 2.4 `Authorization.requireCoordinatorOf` is a static guard with an installed directory

The signature was frozen in E3.5 and its javadoc promised a body "when the repositories arrive". They
have. Implementing it meant giving a static utility a way to reach the `coordinators` table, and I
did it in two halves rather than one:

- `requireCoordinatorOf(caller, subjectCode, SubjectCoordinators directory)` — the real
  implementation, taking the lookup explicitly. **This is what `ApprovalService` calls**, against the
  transaction it already has open, so the feature depends on no process-wide state and its refusal
  path is testable with a two-line lambda.
- `requireCoordinatorOf(caller, subjectCode)` — the frozen two-argument signature, reading a
  directory installed once in `HSTSServer`. **Fail-closed with none installed** (`UNWIRED` answers
  `false`), so the guard's original promise survives.

**What to check:** whether you want the static seam at all, or whether the two-argument form should
be deleted now that nothing but a future call site needs it. I kept it because the frozen signature
is what E6/E9/E11 call sites were told to write against.

The guard also re-checks `requireRole(COORDINATOR)` itself rather than trusting the call site to have
done it. Coordinator-ness is not a stored role (§5) — it *is* a row in that table — so a caller with
a row and a session that says otherwise is a session bug, and a guard that accepted her would be
trusting two sources that can disagree.

### 2.5 Two empty states on one screen

"Nothing is waiting for you" and "you do not coordinate a subject" produce identical zero rows and
mean opposite things. `ApprovalQueue.coordinatesAnything` distinguishes them, and the session picks
the title and hint. It is one boolean on the wire; I mention it because it is the kind of thing that
gets simplified away by a later reader who sees two constants doing "the same job".

### 2.6 The self-approval log line is an interface

`ApprovalMessages.SELF_APPROVAL_MARKER = "SELF-APPROVAL"`, first token of the WARN line, because
acceptance case 4.6 greps the server log for it. Rewording that line breaks an acceptance test that
no unit test would notice, so its shape is pinned by
`ApprovalServiceTest.SelfApproval.selfApprovalIsLoggedInAStableShape` and documented in the contract.
WARN rather than INFO deliberately: the permission is worth nothing without a trace somebody
scanning a log can find.

## 3. The concurrency story, in one place

`exam_versions.status` is the one mutable field of an otherwise immutable row, which is why the table
carries `lock_version`. Three things can move it, and all three are covered:

| racing writer | caught by | answer |
|---|---|---|
| the same coordinator, second window, already decided | status guard (`!isPending()`) | `CONFLICT` + `NOT_PENDING` |
| an E8.2 supersede between her open and her click | **explicit `lockVersion` check** | `CONFLICT` + `DECISION_RACED` |
| another writer committing between our read and our flush | `@Version` → `StaleStateException` | `CONFLICT` + `DECISION_RACED` |

The middle row is why the explicit check exists as well as the flush. A supersede is a **bulk
UPDATE** and bulk updates do not go through `@Version`, so a coordinator holding a superseded row
would have a *current* lock version and would be caught only by the status guard — which is correct,
and is exactly what `ApprovalServiceTest.Supersede.supersededVersionCannotBeApproved` pins. The
explicit check earns its place on the other path: it makes the answer identical whichever writer
moved the row, and it fires before anything is written rather than at flush time where the rule that
caused it is off the stack.

The client's response to `CONFLICT` is **reload, not retry** (`ExamPreviewSession.settleDecision`),
and the reload deliberately *keeps* the refusal message on screen — a screen that quietly repainted
and said nothing is how somebody presses the same button twice.

The real two-connection race is proved against MySQL in
`ApprovalRepositoryMySqlTest.theSecondDecisionLoses`; H2 would let the assertion be written and would
not be evidence.

| method | returns | consumer |
|---|---|---|

`findCoordinatorOf` returns an `Optional`, not a list, because the `coordinators` primary key is the

| method | returns | consumer |
|---|---|---|

By exam version and in `ordinal` order, which is what distinguishes it from `findVersionsForGrading`
(by ids, unordered, because a grader pairs by id). `CorrectnessLeakGuardTest`'s spelled-out inventory
of key-bearing reads was updated — that edit is the deliberate act the test is designed to force.

### New projection

`server/db/projections/ExamVersionContext` — 17 components joining version + exam + course + author,
carrying `subjectCode` (the scoping key) and `lockVersion` (the compare-and-set marker). **No
questions, no answer key**, on the same E2.12 split `ExecutionContext` follows.
