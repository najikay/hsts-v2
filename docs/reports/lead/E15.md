## 3. The extensibility claim, made structural ⚑

F9.4's requirement is a claim about *future* work — "a new report type is a new strategy class plus
a menu entry, nothing else" — which is the easiest kind of claim to make and the easiest kind to be
wrong about. It is proved here in two independent ways, because either alone is satisfiable by a
design that fails the other.

**Behaviourally.** `ReportEngineExtensibilityTest.Fourth` is a complete dimension strategy that
exists only inside that test file. It is handed to a *real* `ReportEngine` beside the shipped ones
and produces a real report — subjects, rows, frozen statistics, pooled summary — through code
written before it existed. It needed **only its own class**: it reused the seam it was handed.

**Structurally.** The same suite reads `ReportEngine.java` and fails if `BY_TEACHER`, `BY_COURSE`,
`BY_STUDENT` or any strategy class name appears in it, and reads `HSTSServer.java` and fails if the
assembly names a strategy rather than the list. A behavioural test alone would pass over an engine
that special-cased the shipped three and fell through to a generic path.

That second test earned its keep during this pass: it caught the phrase
`if (dimension == BY_COURSE)` in the engine's own **javadoc**, which is exactly the kind of thing
that would have made a reviewer believe there was a switch.

### What a fourth report type would touch

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `common/dto/report/ReportDimension.java` | one constant, with its two labels |
| `server/features/reports/BySomethingStrategy.java` | **new**, about 40 lines |
| `server/features/reports/ReportStrategies.java` | one line in `all()` |
| `server/features/reports/ReportData.java` | three methods, *only if* it needs a population the seam cannot already produce |
| `server/features/reports/JpaReportStore.java` | their implementations, one line each |
| `server/db/repos/ExecutionRepository.java` | one query and one `ReportGrouping` constant |

**Untouched:** the engine, every other DTO, both handlers, the summary arithmetic, the whole screen
(its segments are `ReportDimension.values()`, its picker prompt is `subjectNoun()`), the CSS and the
server assembly.

I am stating the `ReportData` cost rather than hiding it. The seam is three explicit methods per
dimension instead of one `executionsFor(dimension, id)`, and that is deliberate: a switch inside
the store would move the dimension knowledge out of the strategies (one small tested class each)
and into the data layer (one growing shared method), which is the shape S-37 asks us not to build.
Six lines in two files is the honest price of that, and the demonstration above shows a strategy
that pays none of it because the seam already served it.

## 4. The summary arithmetic ⚑ — mean of means is dishonest

The obvious summary of a comparison table is to average its columns. It is wrong whenever the
sittings differ in size, which is always. **Eight students averaging 72.5 and four averaging 65 do
not average 68.75; they average 70.** The difference is small, plausible, and permanent once
printed on a principal's report.

`ReportSummary.across` therefore aggregates from what the rows carry:

| Component | Arithmetic | Exact? |
|---|---|---|
| `mean` | `Σ(μ_i × n_i) / Σn_i` | **exact** — each stored mean is its own score total over its own population, so the weighted sum *is* the total of every score in the report |
| `standardDeviation` | `σ² = Σ n_i(σ_i² + μ_i²) / N − μ²`, divisor `n` throughout | **exact** — `n_i(σ_i² + μ_i²)` is row `i`'s sum of squares, and sums of squares add |
| `deciles` | summed per bucket | exact; total is `scored` by construction |
| `passCount` / `passRate` | summed stored numerators over `scored` | exact; the pass mark of 55 is applied nowhere in the class |
| `min` / `max` | min of mins, max of maxes | exact |
| `medianBucket` | the decile holding the `⌈scored/2⌉`-th lowest score | a **band**, see below |

**There is no pooled median, on purpose.** A median cannot be recovered from medians: two sittings
with medians 60 and 80 can have a combined median anywhere between them. Averaging them would
produce a number with no referent — the same dishonesty as the mean of means, without even an
approximate defence. What the rows *do* carry is the pooled distribution, and that pins the median
to a decile. The card prints "70-79" and the hint under it says "band the middle score falls in".
Saying "the middle score is in the 70s" is a claim the stored data supports; "the median is 71.3"
is not.

**Every card's hint names its arithmetic**, and that is load-bearing rather than decorative.
"Weighted by participants" under the average is what stops a reader checking it against the mean of
the column above and concluding the report is broken. The two genuinely differ, and the weighted one
is the true one.

**The hand-computed fixture** (`ReportDtoTest.SummaryArithmetic`, and again end to end on both
engines in `JpaReportStoreContract.serviceOverRealData`): seeded execution 4821 (45, 55, 60, 70, 75,
85, 90, 100) pooled with a second sitting (50, 60, 70, 80).

- 12 scores totalling 840 → mean **70** exactly. `isNotEqualTo(68.75)` is asserted.
- Σ(x−70)² = 2500 + 600 = 3100 → σ = √(3100/12) = **16.0728**. The sample divisor gives 16.79 and
  is asserted *not* to be the answer (H14.4 ⚑).
- 10 of 12 passed. Sixth-lowest is 70 → band **70-79**. Pooled deciles `[0,0,0,0,1,2,2,3,2,2]`.

Plus: pooling two copies of one class must not widen its spread (asserted); row order changes no
figure (asserted); zero variance cannot become NaN (clamped, asserted); a nulls-in-the-list call
fails loudly.

**Is aggregating a recomputation?** No, and the distinction is the same one the lead already ruled
on for `passCount`: this is arithmetic on two stored numbers, not a re-derivation from the rows
travelling beside them. Nothing on this path can reach a grade row — `ReportData` has no method
that returns one.

## 5. Decisions

**Statistics travel as frozen, and `FrozenStatistics` was widened rather than copied.** It is a
three-line constructor call, which is exactly why a second copy was tempting — and exactly how a
second pass mark or a second decile-width check gets into a build. The malformed-column rule, the
reconstituted `count` and the `passCount` arithmetic are decisions, and they now have one home with
two callers. A sitting reading 17.5 on the teacher's histogram and something else on the principal's
report is the failure both classes exist to prevent.

**One `REPORTABLE` clause, three queries.** `ex.status = CLOSED and ex.stats is not null`, written
once in `ExecutionRepository` and appended to by each population query. Equality with CLOSED rather
than inequality with CANCELLED, so the exclusion survives a fifth status.

**H15.2 ⚑, tested where it can actually fail.** Every cancelled-exclusion test builds a **cancelled
sitting with statistics frozen on it** — something the seed does not contain and the ordinary flow
would never produce. It is the only fixture that can tell "cancelled is excluded" apart from
"cancelled sittings have no statistics anyway", and it is used in the unit tests, on H2 and on
MySQL. Three assertions: not in the rows, not in the picker's count, and cannot move the summary.

**The role gate is the whole authorization, and that is unusual enough to say out loud.** Every
other read feature composes a role check with a scope. The principal's scope is the school (spec
7.3.1, F9.3), so a scope check here would be a guard that could only ever pass. Teachers,
coordinators and students get `FORBIDDEN` from the gate rather than an empty list, because an empty
list would read as "there are no reports" — a false statement about the school — and would leave a
teacher clicking a screen that will never fill.

**S-7 is structural, not procedural.** `ReportData` is read methods only. There is no expression in
`server/features/reports` that could change a row, and adding one means adding a method to that
interface first, in a file whose javadoc says why it has none.

**A subject with nothing to compare is `OK` with no rows, never `NOT_FOUND`.** "This teacher has
had no exam close" is a true answer to what she asked.

**`BY_STUDENT` compares the classes, not the child.** Every row carries the sitting's frozen class
statistics, exactly as the other two dimensions do — never her own score. The principal's role is a
read of school-wide statistics; one student's marks are F9.1's screen, gated on being her. The
dimension answers "which sittings was she in, and how hard did each turn out to be".

**Participants ≠ scored.** `ReportRow.participants` is a `COUNT` over attempts, so it counts the
student whose paper was never marked. The gap is printed ("2 unmarked" on the Results card) rather
than quietly closed.

**Subject counts live in the picker.** Every teacher, course and student is listed school-wide, each
carrying how many reportable sittings it has. That turns E15.5's degenerate case into a label before
the click instead of an empty table after it, and the picker opens on the first subject that has
something to compare.

**Rows are oldest first.** A comparison across time reads left to right; a picker's "newest first"
would put the trend backwards. The chart opens on the *most recent* row, which is what a principal
is looking for.

## 6. Deviations, stated ⚑

1. **No grouped bar chart across rows** (E15.4's wording asked for one). The comparison is the rows
   table plus `StatChart` on the selected row's decile distribution. A grouped bar of three numbers
   over two or three sittings carries less information than the table row it would sit under, and
   the decile view — which the requirement also names — is the one that shows shape. **Flagged for
   the lead**; if the grouped bar is wanted for the defence it is a component, not a redesign.

2. **`BY_TEACHER` keys on the exam's author, not on who released the sitting.** E14's precedent and
   E14's reasoning: the frozen statistics describe how a paper was answered, and the paper belongs
   to whoever wrote it. On the seed both readings give the same answer, so this is a decision to
   make now rather than one the data will force. The other reading is a legitimate **fourth
   strategy** keyed on `created_by`, not a change to this one. **Flagged for the lead at freeze**
   (contract §6.1).

3. **E15.1 and E15.2 are not claimed.** See §7.

## 8. Repository additions, for Member A

Added under TEAM_SPLIT rule 5 — repositories grow with their callers — each with its consumer named
in its javadoc, and each contract-tested on **both engines** in `JpaReportStoreContract`.

`ExecutionRepository`:

- `findReportRowsByAuthor(session, authorId)` → `List<ExecutionReport>`
- `findReportRowsByCourse(session, courseCode)` → same
- `findReportRowsByStudent(session, studentId)` → same, membership by attempt (`exists`, not a
  join, so a duplicated attempt row could not double-count her)
- `countReportableBy(session, ReportGrouping)` → `Map<String, Integer>`, one grouped query per
  dimension; keys are `ReportSubject.id` (decimal user id, or course code)
- `ReportGrouping` — a nested enum holding the three grouped queries, so their identical result
  mapping is written once
- `REPORTABLE` / `REPORT_ROW` / `OLDEST_FIRST` — private query fragments; **`REPORTABLE` is the
  H15.2 clause and the one place it lives**

`UserRepository`:

- `findByRole(session, UserRole)` → `List<PersonRef>` — projected, not loaded: a subject picker has
  no business carrying a password hash and a national id (S-6)
- `findRefByRole(session, userId, UserRole)` → `Optional<PersonRef>` — resolves **only** under the
  requested role, so "student 2" cannot come back with a teacher's name

`CourseRepository`:

- `findAllSummaries(session)` → `List<CourseSummary>` — the one read here with no user in its
  signature, because the principal's scope is the school and a caller-id parameter would be a scope
  this feature does not have
- `findSummary(session, courseCode)` → `Optional<CourseSummary>` — the name *and* the code, so two
  similarly-named courses are distinguishable in a picker

New projections: `ExecutionReport`, `PersonRef`.

**Nothing existing was changed.** No signature moved, no query was edited, and `FrozenStatistics`
gained visibility without gaining behaviour.

## 10. What the lead double-checks

In order of how expensive they would be to get wrong:

1. **§4's σ identity.** `σ² = Σ n_i(σ_i² + μ_i²) / N − μ²` is exact for the population form and
   *only* for the population form. If anybody ever changes the stored σ to the sample divisor, this
   line becomes wrong in a way that still produces a plausible number. The hand-computed fixture is
   the guard; keep it.
2. **The author-versus-runner ruling** for `BY_TEACHER` (deviation 2 in §6, contract §6.1). The seed cannot
   distinguish the two, so this needs a decision rather than a test.
3. **`FrozenStatistics` is now public.** That is deliberate and the javadoc says why. If a third
   consumer appears, it should call this and not copy it.
4. **The median is a band and the card says so.** If anybody "improves" it into a point value, the
   number will have no referent. The hint string `MEDIAN_BAND_HINT` is asserted.
5. **`ReportData` has no write method**, and that is the whole of S-7's structural claim. A reviewer
   should confirm no method on it returns anything mutable-through.
6. **The unusable-column path drops the row.** `ReportEngineTest.unusableRecordIsDropped`. It is the
   one place a report silently shows fewer sittings than the picker counted; the count comes from
   SQL and the drop happens in the mapper. That is defensible (the alternative pulls the pooled mean
   down invisibly) but it is an asymmetry worth knowing about before somebody reports it as a bug.
7. **The grouped-bar deviation** (deviation 1 in §6), if the defence wants it.
