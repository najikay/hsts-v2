## 2. Decisions

### 2.1 Scope is authorship, and it lives in the query

F9.2's first sentence is "results for all exams **she wrote**, even executed by others"
(S-35). That is implemented as a `WHERE` clause on `exams.author` in both reads
(`ExamRepository.findAuthoredSummaries`, `ExecutionRepository.findContextsByExamAuthor`), not
as a check after a wider read. A sitting a colleague released comes back to the exam's author;
a sitting of somebody else's exam is never loaded at all. The detail verb compares
`ExecutionContext.authorId()` against the session's user id before it reads a single grade row.

**This is deliberately narrower than E11's monitor rule.** `ExecutionContext.isOwnedBy` admits
the executing teacher *or* the author, because running the room is a live concern. Reading
results is a question about the exam, and the exam belongs to whoever wrote it. So a teacher who
ran a colleague's exam sees its live monitor and does **not** see its results table. That
asymmetry is the reason this service does not call `isOwnedBy`, and it is the thing to
challenge first if the lead disagrees.

The screen does not hide the consequence: a sitting released by somebody else carries
`releasedByAnotherTeacher` and renders a neutral "Run by another teacher" chip, so an author is
not surprised by a sitting she does not remember scheduling.

### 2.2 Not-yours and not-there are one answer

A non-author asking for an execution gets `NOT_FOUND` with the same sentence an unknown id
gets. Two answers would let anybody holding a teacher session walk execution ids and learn
which are real. The test asserts the two responses are **indistinguishable** (same code, same
message), not merely that both are refused.

### 2.3 Nothing recomputes a statistic

Every figure comes out of `exam_executions.stats`, frozen when the last grade was approved
(F8.5): population σ with divisor `n`, pass mark 55, ten stored buckets. `FrozenStatistics` is
the single mapping from the stored record to the wire, and it computes nothing.

The pin for this is a test that hands the service statistics which **flatly contradict** the
rows beside them — every row scores 100, the stored record says mean 72.5 and σ 17.5 — and
asserts the stored figures reach the wire. A service that recomputed would pass every other
test in the file and fail only this one. That is H14.4 ⚑ turned into a regression test rather
than a checklist line.

**The two components the column does not store.** `ExecutionStats` has no `count` and no
`passCount`; the wire shape (which mirrors `ScoreStatistics`) has both. They are
*reconstituted*, not recalculated:

- `count` = the sum of the deciles, because the distribution is one bucket per scored attempt by
  construction. Counting grade rows instead would be a second source of truth and would drift
  the moment a grade was added after the freeze.
- `passCount` = `round(passRate × count)`. Arithmetic on two stored numbers. Re-applying the
  threshold of 55 to the rows is the forbidden move, because a threshold applied twice can
  disagree with itself while both answers look plausible.

For the seeded 4821 that yields 8 and 7, which is what the acceptance table expects to read as
"7 of 8 (87.5%)".

**A malformed column is a calm screen and a loud log.** A stored record that is not ten
buckets, accounts for nobody, or carries a mean outside 0..100 is answered as *absent* — the
same "grading is not finished" state — plus an ERROR line naming the execution. Letting it
through ends either in an exception on a socket thread or in a chart drawing something untrue.

### 2.4 Unfinished grading is a state, not an error

An execution with no frozen statistics still returns its rows, with none attached, and the
screen says so in the calm copy (`ResultsCopy.GRADING_UNFINISHED_HINT`: what is missing, why,
and what will fix it). Refusing to open it would be a dead end (§4.1) for the most ordinary
situation there is — a teacher looking at an exam she is halfway through marking.

The rows are grades, not attempts, so an unmarked paper is absent rather than a row of nulls.
The gap is stated rather than left to be noticed: "6 of 8 papers marked", from the participant
count against the row count.

### 2.5 Cancelled sittings do not exist here (H15.2 ⚑)

Excluded from the list in SQL, and answered `NOT_FOUND` on the detail verb even to the exam's
author. A run that was called off was never sat and has no results; showing it would render an
empty table a teacher then has to interpret. **Flagged for the lead:** H15.2 is worded about
reports (E15), and applying it to the F9.2 screen is this pass's reading of it. If the lead
wants cancelled sittings visible-but-labelled instead, it is one predicate in
`findContextsByExamAuthor` and one branch in `resultsOf`.

### 2.6 Which sitting opens first

The picker opens on the most recent sitting **whose statistics are ready**, falling back to the
most recent sitting of the first exam that has any. Ordering by recency alone would open the
seeded demo on the live execution 2075 with an empty table, which looks broken on the one path
everybody takes. An exam that was never released keeps its place in the rail with an empty
list and its own explanation, rather than being dropped.

### 2.7 The chart is wired, not rebuilt (E14.3b)

`TeacherResultsSession.chartData()` is the whole mapping and is four lines long: the ten stored
buckets, the stored mean, the stored median, the stored population σ, the stored count. An
execution with no statistics becomes `StatChartData.empty()` — never a zero-filled record,
because the chart's "no results yet" and its "not enough results to chart" are different
sentences about different situations. No chart code was written in this pass.

Two assertions tie the chart to the cards printed above it: `StatChartLogic.summaryCaption()`
must read `8 students · mean 72.5 · median 72.5 · σ 17.5` on the seeded execution, and the
first stat card must read `72.5`. If either side ever re-derives a number, they stop agreeing
and one of the two tests fails.

### 2.8 The toggle and the print pass (E14.4)

The T-10 toggle is a segmented `Table | Histogram` control reusing `.hsts-segmented`, so it
looks like the chart's own count/percent switch rather than a second kind of control on the
same screen. The print pass is modest and says so: one style class that flattens shadows,
drops page padding, and removes the exam rail, because **navigation is exactly what a printed
page does not need**. It deliberately does not force a light palette — a teacher who chose dark
mode and pressed "print layout" wants her columns rearranged, not her theme overruled — and a
real export stays with the report engine (F9.4).

## 3. Repository additions — flagged for Member A (TEAM_SPLIT rule 5)

Six reads added to existing repository classes, in the established style: consumer named in the
javadoc, contract-tested on **both** engines (`ResultsRepositoryH2Test` /
`ResultsRepositoryMySqlTest`, 18 cases), no existing contract weakened, no correctness-suffix
rules touched (none of these reads carries an answer key).

| Class | Method | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `ExamRepository` | `findAuthoredSummaries(session, authorId)` | Her exams, joined to the course and to the **latest** version's name. New projection `AuthoredExam`. |
| `ExecutionRepository` | `findContextsByExamAuthor(session, authorId)` | Every non-cancelled sitting of her exams, newest first. Reuses `ExecutionContext` — no new projection. This is where S-35 lives. |
| `ExecutionRepository` | `findIdsWithStatistics(session, executionIds)` | Which of these have `stats` populated. One query for a whole list, not one per row. |
| `AttemptRepository` | `countAttemptsByExecution(session, executionIds)` | Grouped participant counts; an execution nobody joined is **absent** from the map, matching `countAnsweredByAttempt`'s convention. |
| `GradeRepository` | `countGradesByExecution(session, executionIds)` | Grouped grade-row counts, approved or not. |
| `GradeRepository` | `findResultRows(session, executionId)` | The results table: grade + attempt + student name, by name. New projection `StudentResultRow`. |

Two notes for the post-merge review pass:

1. `findResultRows` is **not** scoped to a teacher, deliberately. Authorship is settled one step
   earlier on the execution, because that is where the fact lives; a second filter here would
   look like scoping while depending on a caller passing the right id.
2. The two grouped counts and `findIdsWithStatistics` all return empty for an empty id list
   rather than building an `in ()`, which is a syntax error on one engine and a full scan on the
   other. Each has a test for that case.

## 6. What the lead double-checks

In order of how expensive they would be to get wrong:

1. **The authorship asymmetry (§2.1).** Results are the author's; the live monitor is the
   author's *or* the runner's. If that is not the intended reading of S-35, it is one line in
   `TeacherResultsService.resultsOf` and one predicate in the repository read.
2. **`passCount` reconstitution (§2.3).** It is `round(passRate × count)`. Confirm that counts
   as "reading the stored figure" rather than as a recomputation — the alternative is adding
   `count` and `passCount` to the stored `ExecutionStats` JSON, which is Member A's shape and a
   migration-adjacent change this pass declined to make alone.
3. **Cancelled sittings excluded from F9.2 (§2.5).** H15.2 is worded about reports; this pass
   applied it here too.
4. **The default selection (§2.6).** It prefers "has statistics" over "most recent". That is
   what makes the seeded demo open on 4821 rather than on the live 2075.
5. **`Routes.all()` order.** `AppArgsAndRoutesTest` uses `containsExactly`, so a merge that
   inserts `RESULTS` anywhere but last fails that test rather than silently reordering a menu.
6. **The new MySQL leaf shares the repository suite's schema.** `ResultsRepositoryMySqlTest`
   uses `TestDatabases.mySql()` like every other leaf; a parallel build against the same local
   MySQL can collide there, and a rerun is the right first response.

## 7. Not done, and recommended

**The seed has no S-35 case.** Every seeded execution was released by the author of its own
exam (`dana.cohen` runs her Algebra exam, `avi.mizrahi` runs his Java one), so the "run by
another teacher" chip and the whole point of S-35 cannot be demonstrated on the seeded data —
only in tests, where it is covered on both sides. The smallest fix is one field in
`ExecutionsSection`: release execution 4 (`2075`, live, exam 101101) as `michal.sharon` instead
of `dana.cohen`. That is a one-word change plus the matching line in the seed document's §9
rules table, and it turns scenario 10 of the acceptance walkthrough into a live demonstration
of the requirement.

It was **not** done here because `ExecutionsSection` and the seed document are Member A's
structure, `SeedDocument`/`SeedLoadedDbTest` verify one against the other, and this worktree is
being ported onto `main` after the parallel E8 work lands — a seed edit is the change most
likely to conflict with it. Flagged rather than taken.
