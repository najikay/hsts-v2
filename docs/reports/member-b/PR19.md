`GRADING_QUEUE_GET` and `GRADING_EXECUTION_GET` on the router. Both had DTOs in the frozen

## ⚑ Why this PR exists at all

I found this on starting E12's screens: the server startup log registers five grading verbs, and
**neither of these was among them**. `GradingQueue`, `ExecutionGrades`, `ExecutionGradingSummary`
and `ExecutionGradesRequest` all existed; nothing served them.

Worth saying plainly for the plan: **"E12 screens" has a server half in front of it.** The grading
queue screen had nothing to load. That changes what the item costs, and the manual pass is
scheduled around screens existing.

Cheaper than it sounds, though. Both verbs assemble from reads that already existed —
`findContextsByExamAuthor`, `countAttemptsByExecution`, `countGradesByExecution`,
`findResultRows` — and both sit behind the teacher gate `GradingHandlers` already owns, so
neither introduces a new authorization shape. One new repository read, below.

## The queue is defined by what it leaves out

Executions that are **closed** and have **at least one grade not yet approved**. Three exclusions,
three different reasons, and each has its own test:

| Excluded | Because |
|---|---|
| Still running | Grading an exam people are still sitting is not a task yet. Offering it invites a teacher to approve half a class and wonder where the rest went |
| Nothing marked | There is nothing to approve |
| Already signed off | It is history, and history belongs on E14's results screen |

That last one is the point of the whole class. **A queue that never empties stops being read** —
it becomes a list of everything, which is what the results screen already is.

Ordered **oldest-closing-first**: the sitting that finished longest ago is the one whose students
have been waiting most, and a newest-first queue buries them.

## One new repository read — TEAM_SPLIT rule 5

| Read | Key? | Consumer named in Javadoc |
|---|---|---|
| `GradeRepository.countApprovedByExecution` | no | E12.5's `GRADING_QUEUE_GET` |

The mirror of `countGradesByExecution` directly above it. Together they are the difference
between "nothing marked yet", "half done" and "finished" — three states a queue must tell apart
and which **one count cannot**.

**In bulk deliberately.** The per-execution alternative is one read each, which is fine for a
queue of four and is exactly the shape that quietly becomes a problem at a term's worth. Three
reads for the whole queue whatever its length, and `countsAreReadInBulk` asserts it rather than
leaving it as an intention.

Same conventions as its sibling: an execution with no approved grades is **absent from the map**
rather than present with a zero, so a caller reads both the same way. Carries no scores and no
correctness data.

## Opening one sitting

**Every grade, approved ones included.** A table that hid the approved rows would shrink as a
teacher worked, and give her no way to check a decision she had just made.

Unknown execution and another teacher's are **one indistinguishable empty answer** — a queue row
is not a capability, and a teacher who guesses an id must learn nothing from the reply. Tested by
comparing the two results to each other, as with every other no-oracle rule in this epic.

The **exam's author** may open it even when a colleague ran the sitting (S-35) — the contract's
rule for every teacher verb, and the reason a colleague running your exam does not lock you out
of grading it.

Rows carry `overrideReason`, because this **is** the teacher wire and the justification is what
she wrote. `StudentGradeRow` v1.1's exam labels stay null: the summary above the table says which
exam it is, once, for every row.
