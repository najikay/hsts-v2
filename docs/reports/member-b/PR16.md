## Why it mattered

Nothing about the pipeline was ever wrong: the sources parsed, the prompt assembled, the fallback
chain worked, and all of that was tested. It was the **volume** that was short, because those
paragraphs were written to prove the schema rather than to answer questions. A thin bot is the
one v1 ghost still able to haunt us, and it is the failure that shows up in front of people who
teach the subject rather than in a build.

## The doc-plus-loader pair, made structural

`SEED_CONTENT.md` §10.1 and `BotSection.java` hold the same eight bodies verbatim, and
`SeedLoadedDbTest.botSourcesMatch` fails when they disagree. Editing sixteen places by hand and
hoping is how they drift.

So I wrote the bodies **once** and generated both files from them. The generator is in the
scratchpad rather than committed — it is a one-off for this edit, not a build step, and a
committed generator would imply the files are derived artefacts that nobody should edit, which
is not true and would be worse.

**Mutation check.** Changing "four ideas" to "five ideas" in the document alone, leaving the
loader untouched, fails `SeedLoadedDbH2Test.botSourcesMatch`. So the pair is genuinely held
together and this PR did not accidentally satisfy the test by regenerating both sides of a check
that was never looking.

## What is in the bodies

Real course material for the course, and nothing else. Expanded along the lines the originals
already took rather than off in new directions:

- **Algebra** — linear equations: fractions and brackets before isolating, both solution methods
  for systems, and the three cases with their geometric meaning and the algebraic signature of
  each (`0 = 0` versus `0 = 5`). Quadratics: discriminant cases, axis of symmetry, vertex form.
- **Calculus** — limits: one-sided limits and why the two-sided limit needs both, the three
  techniques for `0/0`, vertical asymptotes, limits at infinity by comparing degrees. Derivatives:
  the rules including the two people get backwards (product is not the product of derivatives;
  the quotient numerator's order matters), extrema by sign change and by the second derivative.
- **Java** — OOP: why encapsulation is about control rather than accessors, composition over
  inheritance and when inheritance is still right, runtime dispatch, interface versus abstract
  class. Collections: the three interfaces, implementation trade-offs with their costs, and the
  equals/hashCode contract, which is the one that produces a collection that appears to lose
  entries.
- **Databases** — normalization: the three anomalies redundancy causes, 1NF to BCNF, the
  "key, whole key, nothing but the key" rule, and that denormalizing deliberately is legitimate
  where duplicating by accident is not. Transactions: ACID, the three phenomena, the four levels
  defined against them, and deadlock as the practical consequence of locking.

Nothing invented, nothing outside the course. Anything fabricated here would be fabricated by
the bot on stage, under questioning.
