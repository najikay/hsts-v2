## What is on screen

Sittings waiting on her down the left; the chosen sitting's students on the right; bulk approve
and the override dialog above the table. One screen, on the `TeacherResultsView` pattern, because
the queue and the table are one task rather than two.

## The two decisions worth reviewing

**After a write, re-read.** Approving and overriding both re-request the open sitting rather than
patching the rows in place. Patching is faster and is exactly how a screen ends up disagreeing
with the server: an approval can be **partly refused**, an override moves a score *and* a state,
and freezing an execution's statistics happens server-side where the client cannot see it. One
round trip removes all three drift paths. `approvalRereadsRatherThanPatching` asserts the order.

The one exception is deliberate: the `ApproveResult` is kept, because the refused ids are the
single fact a re-read **cannot** report — once the table refreshes, a refused row and an
untouched row look identical.

**The selection is cleared on every re-read.** The matching hazard: a selection that survived a
refresh would let a teacher approve a row whose state changed underneath her.

## The confirmation names the consequence, not the action

"Approve 8 grades?" describes what she clicked. What she needs to weigh is that **eight students
are about to be able to see their marks**, and that approving cannot be undone — overriding an
approved grade answers `CONFLICT` by design. The copy says both, and there is a test for the
singular case, because a confirmation that reads "1 grades" looks like a bug at the exact moment
you want to be trusted.

**Select-all skips already-approved rows.** Re-approving is harmless by contract, but including
them would make the confirmation overstate what is about to happen.

## Two client-side checks that are not redundant

The override dialog refuses a blank justification and an out-of-range score **before the request
travels**. The server refuses both too, and its refusal is the one that matters. This one exists
so a teacher who leaves the reason blank is told before she waits rather than after — and the
justification is the one piece of writing she actually had to do.

Relatedly, the override button is **disabled on an approved row** rather than enabled and then
refused. The refusal would be correct and would still waste that writing.

## Copy that does product work

`JUSTIFICATION_LABEL` says two things on purpose: the reason is **stored with the grade**, and
**the student does not see it**. A teacher who thinks it is private writes something careless; one
who thinks the student reads it writes nothing useful for the audit trail (S-23). There is a test
asserting both halves survive editing.

The adjusted marker keys on the two scores **differing**, not on a final score existing — the
same rule as the student side, for the same reason: approving sets `finalScore` to the auto score
when nobody overrode, so a presence-driven marker would tell a whole class their papers had been
hand-changed.

## Not in this PR

**E12.6's full paper review.** `GRADE_REVIEW_GET` is on the router and unused by this screen: the
override dialog changes a score from the table, which is E12.3's need, but a teacher who wants to
see *why* a student lost marks before overriding has no way to open the marked paper from here.
That is the natural next step and it reuses `CheckedFormView`'s shape with the teacher's gate.

I would rather name it than let the ticked E12.5 imply the whole of E12's teacher side is done.
