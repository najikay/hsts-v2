# `SeedArithmeticTest` — the seed document checks its own arithmetic

**Branch:** `test/b-seed-arithmetic` · **Author:** Member B · **Reviewer:** Naji · **Date:** 2026-08-21

18 tests, no new production code. Every number in `SEED_CONTENT.md` that is derived from another
number in it is now recomputed from the document at build time, through Omar's `SeedDocument`.

## Verification

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| `./mvnw clean verify` | see the CI run on this PR |
| `SeedArithmeticTest` | 18 tests across 5 nested groups |
| **Mutation-tested** | 5 deliberate corruptions, each failed the expected group and only it |

## The problem it closes

The seed document states the same fact twice on purpose — an answer grid *and* the score it
produces, a grade column *and* the statistics frozen from it, a roster *and* a notification
quoting its size. Stating them twice is what makes the document readable and hand-checkable. It
is also what lets the two halves drift, and a drifted seed is the worst defect this project can
carry: everything loads, every test passes, and the demo shows a class average that the class's
own scores do not produce.

Not hypothetical. §9 carried auto-scores of 92 and 78 that **no combination of the exam's
questions can yield**. `N-EXEC-CLOSED-ALG`'s title quoted a mean of 78 after the grades had been
fixed to average 72.5. Both were caught by a person reading carefully. This is the build catching
them instead.

## What is checked

| Group | Recomputes |
|---|---|
| `Composition` | every exam version's points sum to 100; no question listed twice in a version |
| `AutoScores` | every auto score in §9.1/§9.2 from the §x.1 selection grid × §7 answer key × §8.1 points; grid and grade rosters agree; every score is a reachable subset-sum; §9.2 approves nothing |
| `FrozenStatistics` | §9.1's mean, median, population σ, min, max, pass rate and deciles, from the final column via `ScoreStatistics` |
| `Participation` | started/finished/timed_out against the attempt-status column |
| `CrossSection` | §11 notification titles against §9.1's mean and roster, and §9.2's row count |

**Nothing hardcodes an expected value.** A test asserting `mean == 72.5` would only prove someone
typed 72.5 in two places. This proves 72.5 is what the eight grades above it actually average to.
The only hardcoded thing is the *relationship* — that a mean is a mean — which is exactly the
part that must not be read from the document.

One deliberate exception: **the scoring rules are restated in three lines rather than calling
`AutoGrader`**. Invoking the grader would make the test agree with the product by construction,
and what needs checking is that the *document* agrees with the product. So the arithmetic is
stated independently and the two are compared. `ScoreStatistics` *is* invoked, because there the
product's formula choice (population σ, divisor `n`) is the thing under test — open question 4,
and the seed and E14 drifting by a point would look like a rounding bug rather than a different
formula.

## Mutation testing — because green on the first run proves nothing

A consistency test that passes immediately is indistinguishable from one that matches nothing.
Five corruptions, reverted after each:

| Mutation | Expected | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `noa.friedman`'s §9.1 auto 90 → 85 | `AutoScores` | ✅ 1 failure, named the student and both sections |
| §9.1 frozen mean 72.5 → 74.0 | `FrozenStatistics` **and** `CrossSection` | ✅ 2 failures — the notification is coupled to the mean, which is the point |
| Notification title mean 72.5 → 78 *(the real historical bug)* | `CrossSection` only | ✅ exactly 1 failure |
| Exam 2 v1 points 30 → 25 | `Composition` | ✅ |
| Participation `{8,7,1}` → `{8,8,0}` | `Participation` | ✅ |

The third is the one worth noting: it is the defect that actually shipped into the document, and
it now fails the build in isolation, naming the section and the value.

The first mutation is also instructive — 85 *is* a reachable total for that exam, so the
reachability check passed and only the grid recomputation caught it. Both checks are needed;
neither subsumes the other.

## Two accessors added to `SeedDocument` — @Omar

`frozenStats()` and `participation()`. Both were unreadable before: §9.1's stats table and its
participation JSON are the most derived data in the document and nothing could see them.

Written to the file's existing rules — picked **by header shape rather than position** (the same
reason §9.1.1's grid is), throwing with a sentence that says what changed and that the parser
changes in the same commit. `passRate()` additionally checks the cell against itself: `7 / 8 =
0.875` is two statements of one fact side by side, so the fraction and the decimal are compared
before either is returned, and no consumer has to wonder which half to believe.

Your class Javadoc already named `SeedArithmeticTest` as the consumer that "recomputes every
score from it", so I have built what it described. Both accessors are additive and nothing
existing changed.

**Your parser handled both traps I had hit by hand.** My throwaway validator read `11005 **v1**→20`
as 20 points *and* a version marker, giving exam 1 v2 a total of 86; and it matched §9.1 and §9.2
rows across sections, double-counting the five students who sit both. `SLOT`'s grouping and the
per-section scoping in `grades(int)` get both right. Worth saying explicitly, since those were
the two hazards I wrote up for you.

## Findings

**None.** The seed document is arithmetically self-consistent as it stands — every auto score is
what its grid produces, every frozen statistic is what its grades average to, and both quoted
notification figures match their sources. That is the expected result after the score fix and the
batch-2 repairs, and it is worth having proved rather than assumed.

## Not in this PR

differ from its exam's author. It is a doc-plus-loader pair — §9's stated rule is that
`created_by` *is* the author, so it needs the rule reworded rather than a row edited, and Omar's
`ExecutionsSection` constants have to move in the same commit. Better as its own change with him
than smuggled into a test PR.
